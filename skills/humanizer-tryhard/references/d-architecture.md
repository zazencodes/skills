# D. Architecture

1. State what the reader needs from the piece and what job each paragraph performs.
2. Follow the actual argument or sequence of events. Remove stock introductory framing, redundant recaps, and mandatory-looking challenges/future-outlook arcs.
3. Retain structure that helps this genre: a long report may need a summary; instructions may need headings and steps.
4. Check that moving or cutting material preserves evidence, qualifications, and argument steps before E.

## Examples and boundaries

**Given:** A project missed its June release because a supplier delivered parts late. The new date is July 12.
**Defective ending:** “Despite these challenges, the project is poised for a promising future as the team continues its journey of innovation.”
**Revision:** End with the new release date and the supported explanation of the delay.
**Why:** The optimistic ending adds a promise and repeats the setup without helping the reader.
**Preserve:** If the brief is a risk report with documented supplier risks and mitigation plans, keep those sections and their concrete content. The word “challenges” is not itself a defect.

**Given:** A biography mentions one award already explained in the career section.
**Defective structure:** An “Awards and recognition” section repeats the award, followed by a conclusion restating the subject's importance.
**Revision:** Describe the award once where it explains the career.
**Preserve:** A requested CV may need a separate awards list; a long analytical report may need a conclusion that synthesizes evidence into a decision.

**Given:** A short reply already states its observation clearly.
**Defective addition:** “Ultimately, this highlights the importance of adapting to an evolving landscape.”
**Revision:** Delete that sentence.
**Why:** It could end many unrelated pieces. Do not replace it with another generic lesson.
**Preserve:** A final question, joke, emotional beat, or call to action can serve the intended reader. Its function must come from the brief and voice, rather than a compulsory ending formula.

Source coverage: outline-like challenges/future prospects; awards and recognition; historical section summaries. Encyclopedia structure is not a requirement for other genres.
